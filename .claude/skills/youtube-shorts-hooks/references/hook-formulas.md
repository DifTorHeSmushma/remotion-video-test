# Hook Formulas Reference — YouTube Shorts (DIY Smart Code)

> **Channel context:** DIY Smart Code — AI IDEs, coding tools, developer productivity  
> **Format:** YouTube Shorts, vertical 9:16, ≤60 seconds  
> **Sources:** Research compiled March 2026

## The 7 Shorts Hook Formulas

Analysis of hundreds of viral Shorts (1M+ views) found that **96% use three or more simultaneous hook elements in the first 3 seconds**. These seven formulas are the building blocks. Use them in combinations.

Source: [YouTube — I Analyzed HUNDREDS of VIRAL Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sb3-tuDwhJ0)

---

### Formula 1: Pattern Interrupt

**What it does:** Breaks the viewer's scrolling autopilot with something unexpected — exploiting the gap between what the eyes see and what the brain can explain. Pure visual or verbal shock forces an involuntary pause.

**Execution rules:**
- Open mid-action with zero context (no setup, no introduction)
- Use a visual that looks out of place in a standard dev-tool feed (unusual terminal output, broken build, unexpected result)
- Flash 0.3 seconds of an intriguing end state, then cut back to the process
- Use a sudden camera angle change or rapid zoom — static first frames underperform dynamic ones
- Deliver the interrupt in frame 1; anything after second 1.5 is too late

**Template:**
> "[Something completely unexpected just happened / is happening right now]. Here's why."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. *Open on a terminal showing 0 errors after Cursor rewrote 400 lines of spaghetti code mid-screen. No narration. Text overlay: "Wait — it actually worked."*
2. *Flash: a VS Code extension that auto-generates unit tests mid-keystroke. Cut back to blank file. "I haven't written a test in 3 weeks."*
3. *Start on a pull request diff — 847 lines changed, 0 added by human. Text: "An AI just shipped my entire feature."*

**When to use:** Before/after demos, unexpected benchmark results, surprise workflow discoveries

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Novelty detection — the brain's threat/novelty system flags unrecognized visual patterns before conscious thought engages. The "what am I looking at?" moment creates a half-second freeze.

### Formula 2: Direct Promise

**What it does:** States exactly what the viewer will get and why it matters — immediately. Eliminates ambiguity, creates a psychological contract, and signals that every second of this Short will deliver value.

**Execution rules:**
- Be specific, not vague — "This one setting tripled my Cursor autocomplete accuracy" beats "A cool AI trick"
- Include a measurable outcome: a number, a time frame, a percentage
- One promise only — multiple promises dilute the commitment
- Use present tense and active verbs throughout
- Deliver the complete promise in ≤2.5 seconds

**Template:**
> "This [specific thing] [does measurable outcome] — here's exactly how."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. "This one Claude prompt cuts my code review time from 45 minutes to 4."
2. "3 Cursor rules that eliminate 90% of AI hallucinations on real projects."
3. "I set up a local LLM in 8 minutes. Zero cloud costs. Here's the full setup."

**When to use:** Tutorials, how-to walkthroughs, productivity tips, setup guides

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Psychological contract — the direct promise creates cognitive obligation. Viewers who hear a specific, credible promise feel compelled to collect the information before swiping, because the cost of missing it feels real.

### Formula 3: Question Hook

**What it does:** Exploits the brain's involuntary tendency to formulate answers — it cannot hear a well-targeted question without engaging. Works best when the question describes the viewer's exact situation.

**Execution rules:**
- Must be a question the developer viewer is *already* asking themselves — not a generic curiosity trigger
- Must be specific to their exact context (not "do you use AI?" but "are you still copy-pasting between ChatGPT and your editor?")
- Must create genuine open-ended curiosity — yes/no questions generate dead ends
- **Self-identifying variant:** "Are you [exact scenario]?" triggers instant "this is about me" connection
- **Blocked intuition variant:** Pose a question where the viewer's instinctive answer feels incomplete — "Why does your AI-generated code always break in production?"

**Template:**
> "Why does [common frustrating situation in dev/AI workflow]? [One sentence that reframes the problem]."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. "Why does every AI coding demo look perfect but break the moment you try it yourself?"
2. "Are you still manually writing boilerplate in 2026? There's a reason you haven't stopped."
3. "What's the actual difference between a $20/month AI IDE and a free one? I spent 40 hours finding out."

**When to use:** Relatable pain-point content, tool comparison setups, myth-busting, "why does X happen" explanations

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Involuntary answer generation — the brain cannot suppress the urge to formulate a response to a well-targeted question. The self-identifying variant adds emotional resonance: viewers who feel understood don't swipe away.

Source: [Opus.pro — YouTube Shorts Hook Formulas](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas)

### Formula 4: Contradiction

**What it does:** Opens with a claim that seems wrong or counterintuitive, creating cognitive dissonance. The brain resists unresolved contradiction and will hold attention until the dissonance is explained away.

**Execution rules:**
- Challenge a belief that developers in the AI/tools niche actually hold — the more widely believed, the stronger the dissonance
- Make a claim that seems implausible but is provably true (and then prove it within the Short)
- Pair immediately with a rapid proof visual — a data point, a demo, a before/after
- Do not leave the contradiction dangling without resolution; the Short must close the loop
- Avoid vague contradictions ("everything you know is wrong") — specificity is what creates real tension

**Template:**
> "[Common belief in the developer/AI niche] is [wrong / backwards / costing you X]. Here's the proof."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. "The AI IDE with the best autocomplete actually makes you a slower developer. I tracked the data."
2. "I got worse code quality after switching to GPT-4o. Here's the benchmark that explains why."
3. "Copilot is free for students. The developers paying $19/month are mostly wasting their money."

**When to use:** Opinion takes, tool myth-busting, contrarian analysis, "hot take" content

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Cognitive dissonance — the brain experiences genuine discomfort when a stated fact contradicts a held belief. Resolving that discomfort requires watching. The discomfort is proportional to how strongly the viewer held the contradicted belief.

Source: [Opus.pro — YouTube Shorts Hook Formulas](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas)

### Formula 5: Mid-Action

**What it does:** Drops the viewer in the middle of something already happening — no introduction, no setup, no context. The brain immediately asks "what is happening?" and stays to find out.

**Execution rules:**
- Frame 1 must already be mid-process — hands typing, terminal running, tool deploying
- Context arrives naturally through narration as the Short progresses, not before the action
- The action itself must be visually compelling or surprising — mid-action on something mundane does not stop the scroll
- Add a text overlay in the first second to layer a verbal curiosity gap on top of the visual one
- Works best when the end state (revealed later) is clearly worth waiting for

**Template:**
> *[Video opens mid-action]* + text overlay: "[What is happening in one provocative phrase]."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. *Open: AI agent autonomously navigating a file tree, opening files, editing, committing — no narration. Text overlay: "I haven't touched this codebase in 6 minutes."*
2. *Open: Claude outputting a 300-line React component while someone sips coffee. Text overlay: "My job right now."*
3. *Open: A local LLM responding faster than the ChatGPT web interface. Text overlay: "No API. No internet. No bill."*

**When to use:** Process showcases, workflow demos, "a day in my AI setup" content, live builds

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Incomplete action loop — the brain cannot abandon an action it has already registered as in-progress. The absence of context intensifies rather than reduces curiosity, because the viewer has no frame to dismiss what they're seeing.

### Formula 6: Pointed Truth

**What it does:** Surfaces a common, recognizable truth that feels personally directed at the developer viewer — a specific scenario they have lived, a frustration they haven't admitted, or a mistake they're currently making.

**Execution rules:**
- State a truth most developers experience but few openly discuss
- Describe a hyper-specific scenario — generic truths don't land; "you just deployed AI-generated code you didn't fully read" lands harder than "AI code has bugs"
- Keep it brutally specific: a named tool, a concrete situation, a real number
- Do not hedge or soften — hedged truths lose the "oh, that's me" recognition
- Follow the truth with a clear forward path: the Short must offer resolution, not just shame

**Template:**
> "If you're [specific developer scenario], [this specific truth about what's actually happening / what it's costing you]."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. "If you're using 3 different AI tools every day and switching between them, you're losing 2 hours a week to context switching alone."
2. "You probably approved a pull request last week with AI-generated code you didn't fully understand. Here's a faster way to actually review it."
3. "Every developer says they'll learn to prompt properly. Almost none of them do. This 4-minute habit changes that."

**When to use:** Habit-change content, productivity pain points, workflow audits, self-improvement angles

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Self-identification — naming the viewer's exact situation triggers a "this is about me" response that bypasses the automatic scroll filter. The more specific the scenario, the stronger the recognition and the harder it is to swipe away.

Source: [Kontent.ai — The Psychology Behind Scroll-Stopping Content](https://kontent.ai/blog/psychology-behind-scroll-stopping-content/)

### Formula 7: Urgency / Warning

**What it does:** Opens with a stop signal — "stop," "don't," "warning," or a red-flag visual — that triggers alert-state processing. The brain prioritizes danger signals before any other input.

**Execution rules:**
- Pair the warning with something the developer viewer genuinely cares about: productivity, money, security, job security, tool lock-in
- The word "stop" literally signals danger and activates threat-detection before the viewer consciously processes the sentence
- Must tie to a real, specific consequence — vague warnings create no urgency
- Immediately follow with the credibility reason: why *you* know this, or a number that proves the stakes
- Never manufacture urgency around a non-urgent topic — credibility damage is permanent

**Template:**
> "Stop [doing X]. It's [specific cost/consequence]. Here's what to do instead."

**Examples for AI/developer tools niche:**
1. "Stop letting Copilot write your SQL queries directly. One hallucination in production cost a company $340k last month."
2. "Don't upgrade to this AI model version right now. There's a context window regression breaking RAG pipelines."
3. "If you're using the default Cursor system prompt, your context window is 40% smaller than it should be."

**When to use:** Security warnings, breaking changes, costly mistakes, tool deprecations, workflow errors

**Scroll-stop mechanism:** Danger/urgency signal — "stop" and "don't" trigger priority processing in threat-detection circuits before conscious thought engages. Combined with a specific stake (a number, a named consequence), the credibility converts the scroll-stop into a commitment to watch.

Source: [Kontent.ai — The Psychology Behind Scroll-Stopping Content](https://kontent.ai/blog/psychology-behind-scroll-stopping-content/)

## Multi-Formula Combinations

Single-formula hooks perform. Multi-formula combinations go viral. These pairings have documented amplification effects.

| Combination | Why It Amplifies | Best content type |
|---|---|---|
| **Pattern Interrupt + Direct Promise** | Visual shock stops the scroll; verbal promise immediately justifies staying. Two cognitive systems engaged simultaneously. | Tool demos, workflow reveals |
| **Contradiction + Direct Promise** | Contradiction creates dissonance; promise resolves it with a specific payoff. Viewer can't swipe without knowing if they're wrong. | Opinion, analysis, benchmarks |
| **Mid-Action + Text Overlay Promise** | Visual curiosity gap from the action + cognitive commitment from the text. Works silently (60%+ of Shorts watched without audio). | Process showcases, live builds |
| **Question + Visual Proof** | Question engages analytical processing; simultaneous visual proof triggers emotional response. Dual-path engagement. | Tutorial hooks, comparison setups |
| **Warning + Specific Claim** | Danger signal (stop/don't) activates alert state; specific claim (number, tool name) makes the danger credible and personal. | Mistake corrections, security alerts |
| **Pointed Truth + Contradiction** | Self-identification creates emotional recognition; contradiction reframes what the viewer thought they knew about their situation. | Habit-change, productivity deep-dives |
| **Pattern Interrupt + Question** | Visual cortex forced into pattern-resolution mode + language center generating an answer. Maximum simultaneous cognitive engagement. | High-energy demos, shocking results |

| **Opinion / Hot Take** | Contradiction | Question | Contrarian claim creates dissonance; a follow-up question invites the viewer to test their own assumption against yours |

## The Scroll-Stop + Commitment Pattern

Stopping the scroll and keeping viewers past the first 3 seconds are **two separate problems** that require two separate techniques. Conflating them is the most common advanced hook mistake.

### Stage 1: Scroll-Stop (First 0–1.5 seconds)

The viewer is in a **passive, reflexive state** — thumb in rhythm, content processed and dismissed before conscious thought engages. The scroll-stop happens when a stimulus breaks through automatic filtering. This is a **pre-conscious event**. The brain does not decide to stop; it stops involuntarily before the decision is made.

What causes a scroll-stop:
- Strong visual contrast (bright/dark, motion/stillness)
- Human faces with extreme emotion
- Motion in frame 1 (the threat/novelty system flags movement automatically)
- Incomplete information — something hinted at but not yet shown
- Urgency signals ("stop," "don't," warning visuals)

The 1.5-second decision window is [confirmed by vidIQ coaching data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u5ZX-GhSots): the retention curve drops almost vertically at 1.5 seconds — what vidIQ calls "the cliff." Your scroll-stop must occur before the cliff.

### Stage 2: Commitment Creation (Seconds 1.5–3)

Stopping the scroll buys 1–2 additional seconds. That window must convert the involuntary pause into a **conscious decision to keep watching**. This requires a different mechanism: a commitment trigger that creates cognitive obligation.

What creates commitment (versus scroll-stop):
- A specific, credible promise — creates a psychological contract
- A curiosity gap — an information deficit the viewer feels compelled to close
- Self-identification — the viewer recognizes their exact situation and feels personally addressed
- A credible warning with specific stakes — the cost of swiping feels real

| Trigger Type | Stops Scroll | Creates Commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Visual contrast | Yes | No — visual alone doesn't create obligation |
| Human face | Yes | Only if expression + audio reinforce a hook |
| Curiosity gap | Yes | Yes — creates cognitive obligation to close the gap |
| Self-identification | Moderate | Very high — emotional investment is built instantly |
| Urgency/Warning | Yes | Yes — if the warning is specific and credible |
| Pattern interrupt | Yes | Moderate — requires a follow-up value signal |
| Direct promise | Moderate | Very high — the psychological contract is explicit |

**The formula:** Build your hook with a scroll-stop trigger *and* a commitment mechanism simultaneously.

Example: "Stop doing this with your AI code reviews" (urgency/warning = scroll-stop) + "it's adding 3 hours to every sprint" (specific stakes = commitment).

## Hook Copywriting Rules for Shorts

These are the language-level rules that apply across all seven formulas. Apply them to every hook before recording.

### 1. Present tense, active verbs
Write as if the action is happening now. "This breaks your context window" beats "This will break your context window." "Cursor generates the function" beats "Cursor can be used to generate the function." Present tense creates immediacy; passive constructions create distance.

### 2. Specific numbers beat vague claims
Every vague claim has a specific version that outperforms it. "Faster debugging" becomes "4× faster debugging." "Most developers" becomes "73% of developers." "A big difference" becomes "saves 2 hours per sprint." Specificity signals credibility — the brain interprets a precise number as evidence of actual measurement.

### 3. Negative framing outperforms positive framing
Research across 105,000 headlines and 370 million impressions found that each negative word in a headline increases click-through rate by **2.3%**, while each positive word decreases CTR by **1.0%**. The mechanism is loss aversion: avoiding a mistake (pain) is a stronger motivator than gaining a win (pleasure). "Stop using the wrong AI model" outperforms "Start using the best AI model." "This mistake is costing you 3 hours" outperforms "This trick saves you 3 hours."

Source: [Nature Human Behaviour — Negativity drives online news consumption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10202797/) (DOI: 10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4)

### 4. Temporal urgency words signal time-sensitive relevance
Words like "right now," "this week," "just dropped," and "before you [do X]" create the impression that the information will be less valuable if the viewer swipes away. They exploit temporal scarcity. Examples in the AI/dev tools niche:
- "The model that just changed everything about local inference — this week only matters"
- "Before you update your Cursor version, watch this"
- "This prompt is working right now — it may not in 3 months"

Source: [SendShort — YouTube Shorts: The Only 5 HOOKS You Need](https://sendshort.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-hooks/)

### 5. Opinionated language outperforms neutral language
Neutral, objective framing reads as "another explainer video." Opinionated language signals a perspective worth encountering. "Cursor is probably the best AI IDE right now" is weaker than "Cursor is the only AI IDE worth paying for in 2026 — and it's not close." Subjective, first-person conviction outperforms careful hedging because it implies the creator has actual skin in the game. Viewers watch opinion; they skim information.

Applied to the AI/dev niche: instead of "Claude and GPT-4o have different strengths for coding," use "Claude destroys GPT-4o for real project work. Here's the test that settled it for me."
