## PART 1: Shorts vs. Long-Form — The Fundamental Difference

Shorts operate under completely different rules than long-form YouTube. Understanding this difference is the foundation of everything that follows.

| Factor | Long-Form YouTube | YouTube Shorts |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Discovery | Viewer clicks thumbnail+title | Auto-play in feed (no click) |
| Hook window | 15–30 seconds | **1.5 seconds** (the cliff) |
| Viewer state | Active — chose to watch | Passive — thumb is scrolling |
| Context-setting time | Available | **Zero** |
| Key algorithm metric | Total watch time (minutes) | Completion % + swipe-vs-view ratio |
| Retention target | >50% at midpoint | **80–90% completion** |
| Orientation | Horizontal 16:9 | **Vertical 9:16** |
| Audio state | Sound on (expected) | Often muted (~60% on mobile) |
| Loop/replay | Not tracked | **Each replay = separate view** |

> **The 1.5-Second Cliff**: The average viewer decides to swipe in 1.5 seconds. The retention curve drops almost vertically at this point. Your hook must prevent that swipe before the viewer's thumb completes the motion.
> Source: vidIQ Viral Shorts Formula — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u5ZX-GhSots

> **The 3-Second Algorithm Gate**: Platforms measure first-3-second retention as a distinct metric. Shorts with 85%+ three-second retention receive 2.8× more views than those below 60%.
> Source: TTS Vibes 2025 Hook Retention Statistics — https://insights.ttsvibes.com/tiktok-first-3-seconds-hook-retention-rate/

> **Brain Activity at Video Onset**: Stanford research (Tong et al., PNAS 2020) shows brain activity in the first 4–8 seconds — specifically Nucleus Accumbens reward activation — predicts aggregate view frequency with 28% explained variance. The hook must trigger the reward system immediately.
> Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104008/

> **Only 45% Get Attention**: A study of 9.2M TikTok recommendations found only ~45% of videos receive meaningful attention. The hook is the mechanism that moves a video from the 55% scroll-past pile to the 45% watched pile.
> Source: Zannettou et al. (2024), ACM CHI — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04945

For the full research base (20+ peer-reviewed papers, Statista data, CB Insights analysis), see `references/research-base.md`.

## PART 2: The 3-Rule Shorts Formula

Based on the vidIQ Viral Shorts Formula (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u5ZX-GhSots):

### Rule 1: THE HOOK — Stop the Scroll (0–1.5 seconds)

All great hooks create a gap between what the brain knows and what it needs to know — before the viewer can swipe. The best hooks are barely verbal; they engineer confusion or curiosity into the first frame.

**Three hook mechanics that work:**
1. **Visual Shock** (Zach D. Films method): Open with a confusing/unexpected visual. The gap between what the eyes see and what the brain can explain IS the hook.
2. **Story Framing** (Dylan Anderson method): Frame a character in a situation that signals something remarkable is about to happen. "The greatest waitress of all time" turned an ordinary clip into 168M views.
3. **Incomplete Information** (Tina Young method): Show an outcome but withhold key information, creating an information gap the brain must close.

**Pattern interrupt every 2 seconds**: Movement, text, zoom, jump cut — anything that visually interrupts the last action. This is the baseline for high-performing Shorts.

### Rule 2: STANDALONE + CHANNEL BRIDGE

The Short must work as a standalone piece AND organically make the viewer want more:
- It must be genuinely satisfying on its own — no cliffhangers without payoff
- The quality of the content itself creates pull toward the channel
- Going to your channel should feel like the viewer's idea, not your instruction
- A cliffhanger without payoff = frustrating. The viewer feels cheated and swipes.

### Rule 3: TRIGGER EMOTION OR ANSWER A QUESTION

This rule drives algorithmic reach because retention is an emotional problem, not a technical one:
- Answer a question people already have (utility)
- OR make people care about questions they didn't know existed (curiosity)
- The algorithm's #1 signal is retention, and retention is driven by emotional engagement

## PART 4: Extracting Short Material from a Long Video Transcript

### Step 1: Scan the Transcript

Read the full transcript and identify:
1. **Core thesis** — The ONE most important claim or revelation
2. **Top 3 surprising facts** — Unexpected results, data, or claims
3. **Best contradiction** — Something counterintuitive the video reveals
4. **Strongest data point** — Most specific, compelling number or comparison
5. **Transformation moment** — Before/after or problem→solution arc
6. **Quotable one-liner** — A sentence that works standalone

### Step 2: Score Each Segment (1–5 scale)

| Criteria | Question |
|----------|----------|
| **Scroll-Stop Power** | Would this make someone's thumb freeze mid-scroll? |
| **Standalone Value** | Does this deliver value in under 30 seconds without extra context? |
| **Specificity** | Does it use concrete numbers, names, or details? |
| **Curiosity Gap** | Does it open an unresolved question the viewer MUST close? |
| **Emotional Trigger** | Does it provoke surprise, curiosity, recognition, or urgency? |

**Minimum threshold**: Combined score of 18+ out of 25 to be Short-worthy.

### Step 3: Structure the Short

```
0–1.5s:  SCROLL-STOP     — The hook. Pattern interrupt + curiosity gap.
1.5–5s:  PROMISE          — What the viewer gets. Direct, specific.
5–45s:   DELIVERY         — Value. Visual change every 2–3 seconds.
45–55s:  PAYOFF           — Answer the question, show the result.
55–60s:  LOOP BRIDGE      — Final frame connects to first frame.
```

### Step 4: Validate

| # | Gate | Pass? |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | **Scroll-stop in 1.5s**: Does the first frame + first sound create a pattern interrupt? | |
| 2 | **Standalone value**: Does the Short deliver complete value without the long video? | |
| 3 | **No setup**: Does it skip ALL context-setting? (No "hey guys", no "today we're going to...") | |
| 4 | **Specificity**: Does it include at least ONE specific detail (number, name, tool)? | |
| 5 | **Text carries the hook silently**: Would a muted viewer understand the hook from text alone? | |
| 6 | **Under 60 seconds**: Does it fit the Shorts format? | |
| 7 | **Loop-ready**: Does the final moment connect back to the opening? | |

All 7 must pass. If any fail, revise.

## PART 5: Vertical 9:16 Visual Design

Read `references/visual-design.md` for the full cinematic layer stack, atmosphere toolkit, and Remotion component code. Key principles:

### The 9:16 Safe Zone

```
┌──────────────────┐  ← Top 20%: Channel name/title UI — AVOID
│                  │
│   ┌──────────┐   │
│   │          │   │
│   │SAFE ZONE │   │  ← Center 55%: ALL critical content here
│   │          │   │     Faces, text, action, key visuals
│   └──────────┘   │
│                  │
└──────────────────┘  ← Bottom 25%: Like/Comment/Share — AVOID
```

### Visual Rules for Shorts Hooks

1. **Center everything.** Eye-tracking research shows central framing yields significantly longer fixation durations in 9:16. Off-center = visual search = wasted cognitive load.
   > Source: Baig et al. (2025) — https://submissions.regionaltribune.com/index.php/trt/article/view/138

2. **Frame 1 must have motion.** Static first frames get scrolled past. Particles, zoom, text animation — something must be alive from millisecond zero.

3. **Visual change every 2–3 seconds.** This resets the viewer's attention loop. A static composition for 3+ seconds = viewer checks out.

4. **Top-to-bottom visual flow** (not left-to-right). Vertical scroll interface means the eye naturally moves vertically.
   > Source: Herasymiuk (2025) — https://theamericanjournals.com/index.php/tajet/article/view/6592

5. **Face-first opening outperforms text-first.** Facial expressions outperform textual sentiment in predicting viewership on short-form.
   > Source: Zha & Chang (2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15278

6. **High contrast opening frame.** Bright against dark. The visual system detects contrast at the lowest level of perception — no conscious processing required.

### The Shorts Cinematic Layer Stack

Same 7-layer architecture as the youtube-hooks skill, but adapted for 9:16:

```
LAYER 7 (top):    LENS SIMULATION      — Film grain, vignette (tighter for vertical)
LAYER 6:          LIGHTING FX          — Light leaks, bloom (edges = top/bottom now)
LAYER 5:          TEXT & CAPTIONS       — Bold, center-screen, within safe zone
LAYER 4:          FOREGROUND            — Close-up fragments, blurred code snippets
LAYER 3:          MAIN SUBJECT          — Screenshots, screen recordings, key visuals
LAYER 2:          MIDGROUND / PARTICLES — Floating particles, bokeh, data streams
LAYER 1 (bottom): BACKGROUND            — Gradient, ambient color, subtle motion
```

**Rule**: Every frame must have visible activity on at least 3 layers simultaneously.

## PART 6: Text & Captions for Shorts

Text is not supplementary — it IS the hook for 60%+ of viewers watching on mute.

### Text Rules

| Rule | Specification |
|------|--------------|
| **Position** | Center third of screen (safe zone) |
| **Font** | Bold sans-serif (Inter Black, Montserrat Black). Min 72px at 1080×1920 |
| **Max words** | 5–7 words per line, max 2 lines visible |
| **Duration** | Each text block: 1.5–3 seconds on screen |
| **Contrast** | Min 7:1 ratio. White on dark + text-shadow/glow. Always readable |
| **Animation** | Word-by-word reveal synced to voiceover (0.1–0.3s ahead of audio) |
| **Hook text** | Biggest, boldest text in the entire Short. Present tense, active verbs |
| **Emphasis** | ONE key word in accent color + slight scale boost |

### Caption Timing Formula
Allow 0.3 seconds per word + 0.5 second buffer. A 5-word caption = ~2 seconds on screen.

## PART 7: Sound Design for Shorts

Long-form hooks build up. Shorts hooks hit on frame 1. There is no ramp-up time.

### The Immediate Audio Rule
The very first sound must signal energy, tone, and intent:
- Music starts mid-energy (NO fade-in, NO silence)
- Impact SFX coincides with first visual
- Voice begins mid-sentence or with high emotional energy
- No "Hey guys" — the first word carries information

### Sound Layers for Shorts

| Layer | What | When | Volume (vs. VO) |
|-------|------|------|-----------------|
| **Immediate Impact** | SFX hit + music beat on frame 1 | 0–0.5s | 30% |
| **Ambient Texture** | Low digital hum, continuous | Entire Short | 10–15% |
| **Transition SFX** | Whoosh on each visual change | Every 2–3s | 15–25% |
| **Accent SFX** | Impact/click on text appears, data reveals | Key moments | 20–30% |
| **Music Bed** | Background energy, consistent | 0s to end | 15–20% |

### SFX Alignment Rule
Peak of sound effect = peak of visual animation. Sound leads or matches — never lags.

### Sound-Off Optimization
- Text overlays must carry the ENTIRE message independently
- Captions in safe zone, always on
- On-screen text matches verbal content, not just supplements it

## PART 8: Loop Design

YouTube rewards Shorts that loop. Each replay counts as a separate view since March 2025.

### How to Create a Seamless Loop
1. **Final frame connects to first frame** — visually or narratively
2. **End strong** — satisfying result that leads back to the opening question
3. **No "end card" energy** — sudden slow-down or wrap-up signals the video is over
4. **The structure rewards rewatch** — first watch = the surprise; second watch = noticing the setup

### Loop-Optimized Structure
```
0s:   Start with a question/claim/visual gap
...
55s:  Deliver the payoff
58s:  Payoff naturally raises the SAME question again → loops to 0s
```

### Retention Graph Diagnosis
- **Cliff at 1.5s**: Hook failed — fix the first frame + first sound
- **Drop at 3–5s**: Promise was unclear after the hook
- **Gradual decline mid-Short**: Content lacks visual variety — add more pattern interrupts
- **Flat/horizontal curve**: Excellent — optimize for loop design
- **Spike at end then replay**: Loop is working

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| "Hey guys, welcome back" | Zero information, zero curiosity. Wastes the cliff. |
| Context before the hook | "So I've been thinking about..." — viewer is gone at 1.5s |
| Dark/blurry first frame | Blends into feed background. No contrast = no scroll-stop |
| Silence or audio fade-in | Missing the immediate audio hook. Sound must hit on frame 1 |
| Horizontal content in 9:16 | Letterboxed video = wasted screen = amateur signal |
| Text in top 20% or bottom 25% | Covered by platform UI. Invisible to the viewer |
| More than 7 words per text line | Unreadable at mobile size. Cluttered |
| Static frame for 3+ seconds | Viewer's attention loop resets. They scroll |
| Cliffhanger without payoff | Viewer feels cheated and swipes. Damages trust |
| "Subscribe" before delivering value | Viewer hasn't received anything yet |
| Vague superlatives ("AMAZING", "INSANE") | No specificity = no curiosity gap. Reads as clickbait |
